# Types of Networks

Networks are broadly classified into four types based on how much geographical area they cover. The four types are listed below:
    1. PAN (Personal Area Network)
    2. LAN (Local Area Network)
    3. MAN (Metropolitan Area Network)
    4. WAN (Wide Area Network)

# PAN (Personal Area Network)

A personal area network has the smallest network coverage area. It is extremely easy to setup and is meant for the use of a single person. It offers low cost with low power consumption, requiring little maintainence. It has obvious disadvantages - limited range and its inability to support a high number of devices.

# LAN (Local Area Network)

A local area network connects multiple devices within a limited geographical area. It is the most common type of network used and can be seen in houses, offices, schools, hospitals, buildings and many more. It offers high speed communication with low latency and high data transfer speed. Its downsides include a high initial setup cost(because of dedicated networking equipment required) and requires administration.

# MAN (Metropolitan Area Network)

A metropolitan area network connects multiple local area networks across a city or metropolitan areas. It is often used by large organizations and offers high speed backbone connections. It is reliable, but way more expensive than a LAN. They require specialized infrastructure to run and are complex to maintain.

# WAN (Wide Area Network)

A wide area network connects multiple networks across countries and continents. The Internet is the largest example of a WAN. It offers global communication, centralized access to data and enables cloud computing. On the other hand, it also carries security risks, has higher latency as compared to the others and has extremely complex troubleshooting.
